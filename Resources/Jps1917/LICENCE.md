# *.usfm — the JPS TaNaKH, 1917

**The Holy Scriptures According to the Masoretic Text**, published by the **Jewish Publication
Society of America** in 1917, translated by a board of editors under **Max Margolis** (1866–1932).
The first English Bible made by Jews for Jews, and the first text in this corpus that is not a
Christian translation.

From <https://ebible.org/engjps/>, read at the source on **2026-09-06**. The archive taken is
`https://ebible.org/Scriptures/engjps_usfm.zip`, whose `sourceDate` in eBible's catalogue is
**2021-11-30**; `scripts/fetch-ebible.ps1 -Only Jps1917` is what fetches it.

**39 books, 929 chapters, 23,145 verses, 611,118 words.** There is no New Testament, which is not an
omission.

**The 1917 and not the 1985.** The New JPS TaNaKH of 1972–1985 is a separate translation, is the
Jewish Publication Society's, and is licensed; eBible's own copyright page points anyone wanting it
at jps.org. The text here transliterates where a Christian version translates — Genesis 22:14 names
the place *Adonai-jireh* — renders the divine name as **LORD**, and sets speech in single quotation
marks throughout.

## What it says about its own numbering

eBible renumbered this text to the English scheme and recorded, verse by verse, the address it holds
at home: **2,056 stated addresses**, written into the running text as `(26-1)`, of which **1,049 are
in the Psalms**, where the Hebrew counts a superscription as verse one and the English does not. They
are read into the `stated_number` rows rather than left in the words.

That matters more here than in any other renumbered text this corpus holds. This is a translation of
the Masoretic text standing beside BHSA, which is numbered the Masoretic way, so the address it
states is the address of the Hebrew it renders.

The file carries **no other annotation**: no Strong numbers, no supplied spans, no marked speech. It
uses nine USFM markers, all already known to the reader.

## Why the statement is believable, and which copy it covers

A 1917 American publication is out of copyright in the United States on the 1929 line, and Margolis,
the last of the board to die, died in 1932, which settles life-plus-seventy as well.

**Whose digitisation it is matters as much as whose translation.** The 1917 text is free; a
particular transcription of it need not be, and two well-known ones are claimed:

- **Mechon-Mamre** asserts copyright over its own digitisation of the same 1917 translation.
- **Sefaria's** JPS directory holds several editions and only one of them is free: the 1917 row
  reads `"license": "Public Domain"`, while its modern JPS editions and its Gender-Sensitive edition
  are CC BY-NC. *Sefaria JPS* is not one thing.

This copy is neither. It is eBible's own, and eBible's public-domain assertion is made on its own
behalf with nobody named upstream of it.
