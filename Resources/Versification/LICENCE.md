## The one term worth reading twice

The header spells out what the licence allows, and one of the bullets is a request rather
than a permission:

> * Include any part of this data in software or publications without requesting permission
> * Download the data and reformat it for your application, without changing the data
> * Refer others to github.com/STEPBible as the source of the data. **Please do not
>   redistribute it yourself.**
>   (Updates or corrections are easier to implement when the data is distributed from a
>   single source)

CC BY 4.0 permits redistribution, so this cannot bind — but RUL-0105 says to read every
statement and take the most restrictive one actually attached to the bytes, and this is
one of them. **This repository honours it**: `TVTMS.txt` is not committed. `.gitignore`
keeps the corpus out, a checkout fetches from STEPBible, and readers are pointed at
github.com/STEPBible as the source rather than at a copy of ours.

The second bullet also asks that the data be reformatted "without changing the data".
Anything the corpus derives from it is written as a derived value beside the source
rather than back into it.

## What it is for

Which verse of one tradition is which verse of another — Hebrew, Greek, Latin and the
KJV's English — with the mapping rules that make them comparable. It is what lets a
reference in one text find the same words in another when the two number their verses
differently.
