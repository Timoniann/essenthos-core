# *.usfm — Young's Literal Translation, 1898

**The Holy Bible, Containing the Old and New Covenants, Literally and Idiomatically Translated out of
the Original Languages**, by **Robert Young** (1822–1888), the compiler of Young's Analytical
Concordance. First published in 1862; this is the revised edition of 1898, printed after his death.

From <https://ebible.org/engylt/>, read at the source on **2026-09-06**. The archive taken is
`https://ebible.org/Scriptures/engylt_usfm.zip`, whose `sourceDate` in eBible's catalogue is
**2019-10-21**; `scripts/fetch-ebible.ps1 -Only Young1898` is what fetches it.

**66 books, 1,189 chapters, 31,102 verses, 786,938 words** — the King James numbering exactly, verse
for verse, with no slot empty and none added.

**Why this text matters more here than its readership would suggest.** Young translated by rule
rather than by idiom: Hebrew verb aspect rendered the same way whatever English does with it, source
word order kept wherever English bears it, one original lexeme rendered by one English word far more
consistently than in any other version. Genesis 1:1 reads *In the beginning of God's preparing the
heavens and the earth*, and John 3:16 ends *may have life age-during*. Neither is idiomatic English
and both are what a word-for-word rendering produces. That awkwardness is a cost to a reader and a
gift to an aligner, which is the reason it is loaded.

Its Greek is the Textus Receptus: Acts 8:37, Matthew 17:21, John 5:4, the heavenly witnesses of
1 John 5:7 and the doxology of the Lord's Prayer are all printed, the last as *Thine is the reign,
and the power, and the glory*.

The file carries **no annotation of any kind** — no Strong numbers, no supplied spans, no marked
speech — and uses nine USFM markers, all already known to the reader. Whatever this text ever
reaches the Hebrew and Greek by will be this project's own alignment, and that is worth saying
plainly rather than leaving to be discovered.
