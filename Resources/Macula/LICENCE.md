## The five works underneath it, and what each carries

`LICENSE.md` is explicit that the repository is a composite, and says where to look:

> In addition to datasets from Clear, MACULA contains data from the following datasets. Where a
> repository is given, please refer to the repository for the relevant license

Read at the source on 2026-09-06, in the order the file lists them:

1. **Nestle 1904**, <https://github.com/biblicalhumanities/Nestle1904> — the text, the lemmas, the
   Strong numbers and the `morph` tag. `morph/README.md` there: *"To the extent possible under law,
   biblicalhumanities.org has waived all copyright and related or neighboring rights to the
   biblicalhumanities.org Nestle 1904 Morphology"*, under CC0 1.0, with the analysis credited to
   Dr Ulrik Sandborg-Petersen and, before him, to Dr Maurice A. Robinson. **This corpus already
   holds that same edition from that same repository** — `../Nestle1904/LICENCE.md` records the
   same reading, made independently on 2026-08-31.
2. **SBLGNT** from Logos Bible Software — the other text MACULA is published over, in the
   repository's `SBLGNT/` folder. **Not fetched, not read and not loaded here.** Its terms are
   TSK-0223's business.
3. **UBS MARBLE** word senses — the `domain` and `ln` columns, *"Used with permission"*, which is
   not a licence anybody else can rely on. **Not loaded.**
4. **The Berean Interlinear Bible** — the `gloss` column, *"officially placed into the public domain
   as of April 30, 2023"*, which is what `../Berean/LICENCE.md` records for the same publisher.
   **Not loaded**, because the Nestle text in this corpus already carries the Berean glosses from
   `berean-interlinear-glosses.xml`.
5. **Cherith Glosses for the Greek New Testament**, Andi Wu, © 2023 Cherith Analytics, CC BY 4.0 —
   the `English` and `Chinese` columns. **Not loaded.**

So of the five, one is CC0 and already here under its own reading, one is CC BY 4.0, one is public
domain, one is permission rather than a licence, and one is a text this corpus does not hold. **What
is loaded is Clear's own layer over the first of them**, which is the CC BY 4.0 part, and the two
columns that matter most — `class` and `type` — exist in none of the five sources: they are the
annotation itself.

## What is taken and what is left

Taken, into `word_parsing` rows against Nestle 1904 words:

| column | why |
|---|---|
| `class` | the part of speech, with `num` and `ptcl` as classes of their own |
| `type` | **the reason this dataset was fetched** — `proper` against `common`, stated per word |
| `lemma` | a second lemmatisation beside the text's own |
| `morph` | the form code, so a reader can see what the features were read from |
| `person`, `number`, `gender`, `case`, `tense`, `voice`, `mood`, `degree` | the features |
| `ref`, `xml:id` | read to place the row and then dropped |
| `text` | read only to check the placement, and then dropped |

Left where it is, each for a reason and each worth its own piece of work rather than a column
smuggled in beside a morphology:

- `role`, `frame`, `subjref`, `referent` — semantic role, semantic frame and participant reference.
  These are claims about clauses and about who *he* is, not about what a word is, and they have no
  home in a table called `word_parsing`.
- `domain`, `ln` — Louw-Nida semantic domains, from MARBLE, which is used by permission.
- `english`, `mandarin`, `gloss` — three glossing layers from two other publishers, where this
  corpus already carries the Berean glosses on these very words.
- `Nestle1904/lowfat/*.xml` — 110 MB of syntax trees, which is the dataset MACULA is actually
  famous for. Not fetched. The word table above is generated *from* those trees, so nothing that is
  loaded needs them.

The two files kept beside this one are the upstream `LICENSE.md` and `README.md` as fetched, because
a copy of a licence that has been edited is no longer the thing that was read.

## No alignment, and it is checked rather than claimed

The row `n40001001001` carries `ref="MAT 1:1!1"`, and this corpus's Nestle 1904 has a word at
Matthew 1:1 position 1. Measured on 2026-09-06 against the loaded corpus: **7,943 verses on both
sides, 137,779 words on both sides, and not one verse with a different number of words in it.**

That is the whole reason this source was preferred, so the loader refuses to write anything unless
it is still true — it compares every verse's length before it writes a row, and stops with the first
verse that disagrees rather than shifting the rest of a book by one.

Two smaller disagreements are expected and are recorded rather than repaired, because both copies
descend from the same upstream repository at different times:

- **193 words are spelt differently**, 150 of them a breathing mark on `Ἡ` against `Ἠ` — Ἡρῴδης,
  Ἠλείας, Ἠσαΐας. Both spellings go into the row's `note`, so the drift is visible per word.
- **224 words carry a different Strong number**, 0.16% — mostly comparatives and superlatives given
  their own number by one copy and the positive's number by the other (`πλεῖον` as G4183 or G4119),
  and adverbs of time (`ποτε` as G4219 or G4218).

## What this data does not settle, so that nobody looks twice

**It covers one witness.** The corpus holds four Greek texts and MACULA is published over Nestle
1904 and the SBLGNT only. Robinson-Pierpont, Scrivener and Stephanus gain nothing from it, so a gate
built on a stated class here still has to be inferred for them.

**Its `morph` column is not an independent analysis.** It is the same Sandborg-Petersen tag this
corpus's Nestle words already carry — identical on 137,435 of 137,779 words. So where MACULA's
`case` differs from ours, it is two readings of one tag rather than two opinions about one word,
which is exactly what makes it useful for checking the reader and useless as a second witness to
the morphology itself. `class` and `type` are the parts that are genuinely Clear's.
