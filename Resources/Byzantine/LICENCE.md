# strongs/*.BP5 — the Byzantine Textform of Robinson and Pierpont

**The New Testament in the Original Greek: Byzantine Textform**, edited by **Dr Maurice A.
Robinson** and **William G. Pierpont**, with morphological parsing tags and Strong's numbers. The
repository is maintained by the ByzTxt organisation — the same one that holds
`greektext-textus-receptus`, already loaded here.

From <https://github.com/byztxt/byzantine-majority-text>, read at the source on **2026-09-03** at
commit **27a45ff1b7be6c17ccbfeac414f3f55732ae8e28**, which is release **v3.3.2**. The text is the
**2018** edition, current as of 20 July 2023.

## The statement that does not exist

**No first-person statement by Robinson placing this edition in the public domain could be found.**
The Unlicense and the README are the maintainers' claim about Robinson's files, not his own about
this edition. What exists in his own words is the permission notice for the Byzantine Textform at
<https://byzantinetext.com/study/editions/robinson-pierpont/>, which contradicts itself in its own
header — the same notice `../TextusReceptus/LICENCE.md` already records:

> This Compilation is Copyright © 2005 by Robinson and Pierpont.

and then, of the same text:

> Anyone is permitted to copy and distribute this text or any portion of this text.

> All rights to this text are released to everyone and no one can reduce these rights at any time.

The release is the operative half. The notice also carries a request, which is a request and not a
condition:

> the present editors' names and the title associated with this text as well as this disclaimer be
> retained in any subsequent reproduction

It is honoured: both editors are named on the `text` row for this edition, in
`Loading/ByzantineTextSource.cs`, and here.

## What is taken

- `strongs/*.BP5` — Robinson's own parsed files, 27 books, 7,957 verses, 140,149 words. Unaccented
  beta code, and every word carries a Strong number and a parse. **This is what is loaded.**
- `unicode/*.csv` — the repository's own Unicode conversion of exactly those files, from
  `csv-unicode/strongs/with-parsing`. Not loaded. It is the answer key: the beta-code table in
  `Byzantine/Bp5BetaCode.cs` was derived from it and is checked against all 140,149 words in the
  tests, the same way the Textus Receptus extraction is checked against `scrivener/*.SCV`.

## What is not taken, and why it is worth coming back for

`source/CCAT` is Robinson's accented text with the critical apparatus inline: the places where the
Byzantine tradition itself divides, and where it parts from Nestle-Aland and the *Editio Critica
Maior*. It is a second edition's worth of stated variation, in the same `{ }` notation the Textus
Receptus composite's variant groups already use. Nothing here reads it yet.

`tei-xml-unicode` and the other CSV forms are derived from the two files above and add nothing this
corpus needs.

## Which of the four representations is authoritative

The repository says it itself: *"The official files produced by Professor Robinson are"* the parsed
beta code and the accented CCAT; *"Professor Robinson's files are the source of truth."* The Unicode
CSVs are generated by the maintainers' scripts. So the beta code is what is loaded and the CSV is
what it is checked against, rather than the other way round.

## Why it is attributed at all

Public domain removes the obligation, not the reason. A reader of this corpus has to be able to
tell what rests on somebody's testimony and what rests on our inference, and a fact printed without
a name has quietly been claimed as ours. RUL-0181.
