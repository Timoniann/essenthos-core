# «Known for» pilot — 30 people (FTR-1109), 2026-10-21

**What this is.** A trial of a «known for» line in a person's one-line summary: one to three short clauses of what the person did or what happened to them, each with its verses. Data only — nothing is loaded and nothing on the site has changed.

**How it was made.** For each person the record (name, current summary) and the verses that name them were read from the live site, read-only. A first reader (Claude Opus 5.5) read the verses in the KJV and wrote the clauses with references, past tense, plain words, only what the text states; then the Ukrainian, with names as the site gives them. A second, separate reader (another Claude Opus 5.5 run, not involved in the first) checked every clause against the KJV text of its cited verses and the Ukrainian against the English. Its fixes are applied; the first wording is kept in `pilot.json` and the raw verdicts in `raw/check.json`.

**Counts.** 30 people, 76 clauses. Second reading: 65 right, 11 fixed (†). Two clauses replaced after its notes on what was missing (‡); three references added where it suggested; one line shortened for length.

**Length** (text without references). English: median 125, shortest 75, longest 159 characters; 14 of 30 within 120, all within 180. Ukrainian: median 115, longest 164; 19 within 120. By band (English): most 101–159, median 142; middling 75–149, median 109; minor 88–119, median 105. The best-known take three clauses; the minor ones two. With references the English runs 102–223 characters.

**Verses** is the number of verses in which the site's data names the person. It undercounts where a person is mostly «he» or «I»: Nehemiah's own book is in the first person, so he shows 5.

## What the second reading changed

- **Moses** † — The only cited verse for the leading out (EXO 12:51) says the LORD brought Israel out, and 14:21-31 credits the LORD with the sea; ACT 7:36 says Moses brought them out with signs in the Red sea. (references were EXO 12:51, 14:21-31)
- **Elijah** † — Elijah prayed (18:36-37) and 'the fire of the LORD fell' (18:38); 'called down' credits him with the act, as 'healed' would where God healed. (English was «called down fire from heaven on Mount Carmel»; Ukrainian was «звів вогонь з неба на горі Кармел»)
- **Peter** † — Pentecost is named only in ACT 2:1, outside the cited range. (references were ACT 2:14-41)
- **Paul** † — The Ukrainian makes Paul the one who met Jesus; the English (and ACT 9:17, 'Jesus, that appeared unto thee') has Jesus as the actor. (Ukrainian was «зустрів Ісуса на дорозі до Дамаску й охрестився»)
- **Paul** † — ACT 13:46-49 is one city (Antioch in Pisidia); 'in many cities' needs a verse such as ACT 15:36, 'every city where we have preached'. (references were ACT 13:46-49, 28:14-16)
- **Joseph** † — 45:4-11 is Joseph's plan to nourish them ('there will I nourish thee'); that he did keep them alive is stated in 47:12, outside the range. (references were GEN 41:47-57, 45:4-11)
- **Gideon** † — The English holds (7:7, 7:20-22), but the Ukrainian numeral phrase does not agree: in the instrumental the noun follows the numeral, so it is «трьомастами чоловіками», not «трьомастами чоловік». (Ukrainian was «з трьомастами чоловік розбив мідіянітян»)
- **Hezekiah** † — 20:1-6 is only God's promise ('I will heal thee'); the recovery itself is 20:7. (references were 2KI 20:1-6)
- **Josiah** † — 'Idols' are named in 2KI 23:24 ('the images, and the idols ... did Josiah put away'), outside 23:4-20. (references were 2KI 23:4-20)
- **Josiah** † — «загинув від фараона» is not natural Ukrainian; the death needs «від руки». (Ukrainian was «загинув від фараона Нехо в Меґіддо»)
- **Martha** † — «прийняла ... у своєму домі» does not render 'received into her house', and «клопоталася про багато служіння» is not natural Ukrainian. (Ukrainian was «прийняла Ісуса у своєму домі і клопоталася про багато служіння»)
- **Moses** ‡ — «was called by God from a burning bush» (EXO 3:1-10) replaced «died in Moab after seeing the promised land from afar» (DEU 34:1-5), after the second reading noted the more central event was missing. Checked against the verses by the first reader only.
- **Elisha** — shortened after the check for length (was: followed Elijah and succeeded him, and Elijah's spirit rested on him); same verses, same sense.
- **Samuel** ‡ — «judged Israel all his life» (1SA 7:15-17) replaced «was given to the LORD by his mother as a child» (1SA 1:24-28), after the second reading noted the more central event was missing. Checked against the verses by the first reader only.
- **Samuel** — added 1SA 15:1, as the second reading suggested.
- **Solomon** — added 2CH 3:1, as the second reading suggested.
- **Nehemiah** — added NEH 1:3, as the second reading suggested.

## Selection notes not acted on

The second reading's remarks on what else could be chosen, left for you to weigh:

- **Abraham**: Clause 2 could carry GEN 15:6 ('he believed ... counted it to him for righteousness'), which is the verse the NT quotes.
- **David**: His sin with Bathsheba and the death of Uriah (2SA 11-12) is a defining event and is missing; Samuel's anointing of him (1SA 16:13) is another.
- **Deborah**: Fine; a reader may note that Jael, not Deborah or Barak, killed Sisera (4:21).
- **Stephen**: His speech before the council (ACT 7:2-53) is what most of his story consists of.
- **Josiah**: His Passover (2KI 23:21-23) is a further candidate, but the selection is sound.

## Seen along the way

- Some people are known for one act only (Enoch, Rahab, Naboth): two clauses is the natural size, and padding to three would add trivia.
- Women named through men: for Sarah, Ruth, Abigail and Martha the line can still say what *they* did (gave Hagar, laughed and bore Isaac; went with Naomi; brought food to David and stopped him; received Jesus, confessed him), which the current summary — all «wife of», «mother of», «sister of» — never says.
- Peter's Ukrainian name comes from the site as «Петр» (with «Симон» as the other name); Ukrainian Bibles have «Петро». Sarah's and Rahab's current Ukrainian summaries say «похований у Махпелі» and «жив у Єрихоні», masculine for a woman.
- Samuel's «judged Israel» repeats the «judge of Israelites» already in his summary; where an office already says it, the line could keep to deeds.
- Choosing is the hard part for the best-known: David, Moses, Peter and Paul each have more than three defining events, and which three is a judgement (see the notes above).
