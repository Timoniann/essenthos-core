# *.usfm — Tyndale's New Testament, 1534

**The New Testament**, translated by **William Tyndale** (c. 1494–1536) from Erasmus's Greek. The
first English New Testament made from the Greek and the first printed in English at all; the text
the King James committees of 1611 largely revised rather than replaced.

From <https://ebible.org/engtnt/>, read at the source on **2026-09-06**. The archive taken is
`https://ebible.org/Scriptures/engtnt_usfm.zip`, whose `sourceDate` in eBible's catalogue is
**2019-10-23**; `scripts/fetch-ebible.ps1 -Only Tyndale1534` is what fetches it, and it re-reads
every statement below before it replaces anything.

**27 books, 260 chapters, 7,957 verse slots of which 7,954 hold words, 181,998 words.** The three
empty slots are Mark 11:26, Luke 17:36 and Revelation 21:26. They are not a defect of the download:
Stephanus first divided the New Testament into verses in 1551, seventeen years after this printing,
so the numbering here is a later division fitted onto an earlier text and a few of its slots have
nothing of Tyndale's to hold.

**The spelling is Tyndale's own and has not been modernised.** That was checked rather than assumed,
because several digital Tyndales are modern-spelling editions and a modern-spelling Tyndale is close
enough to the King James to be worth nothing beside it. Matthew 1:1 reads *This is the boke of the
generacion of Iesus Christ the sonne of Dauid*, and John 3:16 *For God so loveth the worlde that he
hath geven his only sonne* — u for v, i for j, and his orthography throughout.

The file carries **no annotation of any kind**: no Strong numbers, no lemmas, no morphology, no
alignment, and no marked spans. It uses nine USFM markers and all of them were already known to the
reader.

## Why the statement is believable

Tyndale was strangled and burned at Vilvoorde in 1536, so the translation has been out of copyright
in every jurisdiction for centuries and the only question a modern edition can raise is whether
*this transcription* is somebody's work.

It is worth asking, because CrossWire reads one source two ways. Its `tyndale` module and its
`wycliffe` module were both made from Wikisource transcriptions, and it stamps the first
`DistributionLicense=Public Domain` and the second `Creative Commons: BY-SA 4.0`. Both readings of
one source cannot be right about it, so neither is worth relying on. eBible's assertion is made on
its own behalf, with nothing named upstream of it, and it is the one taken.

**CrossWire's `tnt` is not this text.** It is Tregelles' Greek New Testament — Tyndale *House*,
Cambridge, the institution and not the man — `Lang=grc`, `DistributionLicense=Creative Commons:
BY-NC-SA 4.0`. ShareAlike, and Greek. Recorded here so nobody takes it by mistake.
